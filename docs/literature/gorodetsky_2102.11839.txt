New representations for all sporadic Ap´ ery-like sequences, with
applications to congruences
Oﬁr Gorodetsky
Abstract
We ﬁnd new representations, in terms of constant terms of pow ers of Laurent polynomials, for all the
15 sporadic Ap´ ery-like sequences discovered by Zagier, Al mkvist-Zudilin and Cooper.
The new representations lead to binomial expressions for th e sequences, which, as opposed to previous
expressions, do not involve powers of 3 or 8. We use these to es tablish the supercongruence Bnpk ≡
Bnpk− 1 mod p2k for all primes p ≥ 3 and integers n, k≥ 1, where Bn is a sequence discovered by Zagier,
known as Sequence B.
Additionally, for 14 of the 15 sequences, the Newton polytop es of the Laurent polynomials contain the
origin as their only interior integral point. This property allows us to prove that these sequences satisfy
a strong form of the Lucas congruences, extending work of Mal ik and Straub. Moreover, we obtain lower
bounds on the p-adic valuation of these sequences via recent work of Delayg ue.

2.3 p-adic valuation of sporadic sequences

3.4 New constant term representations
The discussion in §3.3 shows that there are known constant term representations f or A, B, C, F, (γ) and ( α),
and also that a previously known diagonal representation for D gives rise to constant term representation
through Lemma 3.2.
We call a Laurent polynomial good if it may be factored into a product of Laurent polynomial, each of
which has coeﬃcients in the set {−1, 0, 1}. We have conducted a computer search of the following form: we
enumerated good Laurent polynomials f (in several variables) with bounded degree and coeﬃcients. For ea ch
such polynomial we tested whether CTS( f ) is the g.f. of a sporadic sequence. For each of the 15 sequences,
the search yielded more than one Laurent polynomial whose CTS is its g.f.. The reason for looking only at
good Laurent polynomials is that they lead to binomial sum represent ations which do not involve powers of
integers other than ±1 (as in Proposition 1.2).

6 Future directions
1. Suppose an integer sequence {un}n≥0 is given in terms of a binomial sum, or as the diagonal of rational
function. Is there an algorithm deciding whether un = CT(Λ n) for some Laurent polynomial Λ, and
for ﬁnding such a Λ? Is there an eﬃcient algorithm which produces Λ s uch that the only integral point
in its interior is the origin, in case such Λ exists?
Positive answers to these questions would lead to an alternative pro of of Theorem 1.1. The ﬁrst
question is a special case of a question raised by Zagier [58, p. 769, Q1], asking how to recognize that
a diﬀerential equation has geometric origin.
2. Fix d ∈ {2, 3}. Can one classify integer sequences {un}n≥0 such that y =∑

d = 2) or (1.7) (if d = 3), and in addition un = CT(Λ n) for some Laurent polynomial Λ in d variables?
Such a classiﬁcation might give support to the conjecture that the 15 sporadic sequences that were
discovered are the only integral solutions to (1.6) and (1.7).
3. Can one ﬁnd new Calabi-Yau equations by considering the annihilatin g operator of CTS(Λ) for various
good Laurent polynomials Λ? Here ‘good’ is used as in §3.4.
