\begin{abstract}
Historical attempts to describe cosmology through transfinite geometry have frequently foundered on non-dynamical numerology. However, the profound underlying intuition---that macroscopic gravitational phenomena are manifestations of shifting topological phases---finds exact realization in modern String Phenomenology. We present Topological Phase Cosmology, a dynamically evolving global theory of the Dark Sector based on Spontaneous Geometric Symmetry Breaking. By coupling the local stress-energy tensor to the Picard-Fuchs moduli space of a K3-fibered Calabi-Yau compactification, we demonstrate how baryonic overdensities trigger continuous topological phase transitions between the Order-3 K3 surface ($S_{1,2}$) and Order-2 elliptic ($S_{2,1}$) geometries. This framework is observationally anchored by a converged GPU tensor analysis of 327,918 SDSS DR17 galaxies performed by the companion \texttt{DarkMatterK3} pipeline (analysis artifacts external to this repository; see \S\ref{sec:provenance}), which reports a macroscopic perturbative bound of $S_{1,2} \le 1.177$ and isolates a 22-node supercluster intersection peaking at an asymmetry of $\Delta = 47.0$. We frame these as pipeline-reported consistency statements, not independent empirical proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Empirical Status, Provenance, and Physical Interpretation}\label{sec:provenance}

\subsection{Provenance of the quoted statistics}
The four headline numbers in this paper --- the 327,918-galaxy sample, the bound $S_{1,2} \le 1.177$, the phase-transition threshold $S_{1,2} \ge 1.8$, and the K3-DISC-0003 spike $\Delta = 47.0$ --- are outputs of the companion \texttt{DarkMatterK3} / K3@Home GPU tensor pipeline. The committed code and data artifacts of the present repository contain the pipeline's \emph{algorithmic components} (topological FFT, Poisson null-hypothesis harness, tensor-grid projection in \texttt{lss\_tensor\_analytics/}) and a 500-galaxy live-query KK projection (\texttt{data/ws9\_sdss\_kk\_map.csv}), but not the full converged run behind these four numbers. Until that run is archived with a reproduction script, these values carry the epistemic status of \emph{reported pipeline outputs}, and every interpretive statement below is conditional on them. The threshold $1.8$ is additionally a model-internal definition (the value of the order parameter at which the perturbative expansion of the phase-transition free energy is deemed to break down), not an independently derived critical point.

\subsection{Freeze-out: the reconciliation with the 5D resonance framework}
Lee \& Tsai's orbifold model requires dark matter that interacted strongly in the dense early universe but is nearly inert today. Within our framework the two constructions occupy complementary roles: the $S^1/(Z_2 \times Z_2')$ orbifold supplies the \emph{microscopic} mass-generation mechanism (KK quantization against the compactification radius $R$), while the TDA pipeline supplies the \emph{macroscopic} observable (the density-coupled order parameter $\Delta$, with the local baryonic field ``pinching'' the effective $R$). No browser-side simulation of the orbifold itself is required --- or possible --- in this division of labor: the orbifold physics enters the observatory only through the parameterized response $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta)$, and the citizen-science pipeline's task is to measure the macroscopic statistic, not to solve the 5D field equations. Under this reading, the reported bound $S_{1,2} \le 1.177$ is \emph{consistent with} --- though it does not by itself prove --- the frozen-out phase the 5D framework requires at $z \approx 0$: a locally active resonance would have manifested as sectors approaching or exceeding the transition threshold.

\subsection{Sector-level stability of the local vacuum}
The pipeline's sector decomposition reports $S_{1,2} < 1.3$ across all analyzed sky sectors, uniformly below the $1.8$ transition threshold. The physically expected reading is that galaxy groups and satellite systems are \emph{macroscopically non-resonant}: a full topological phase transition sustained across a $\sim$10-degree sector of the local volume would correspond to a non-perturbative geometric instability, which manifestly does not occur. The margin between $1.177$ and $1.8$ (a $\sim$17.7\% maximal warping relative to the symmetric point, against a $\sim$80\% transition criterion) quantifies --- conditionally on the pipeline definitions above --- the stability of the late-time ($z < 0.5$) vacuum, and relegates extreme resonances to micro-environments (early-universe direct collapse, black-hole horizons) exactly as the See-Saw picture requires. We stress the epistemic asymmetry: a sector \emph{violating} the bound would have been a striking positive signal; its absence is a consistency check, not a discovery.

\subsection{The $T^2$ proxy versus Poisson mocks: an algorithmic, not cosmological, victory}
In null-hypothesis runs (cf.\ the in-repository harness \texttt{lss\_tensor\_analytics/null\_hypothesis\_test.py}), shuffled Poisson mock catalogs yield a \emph{higher} $T^2$ topological proxy than the real galaxy data. This direction is informative: uniform noise fragments into many small spurious components and noisy tunnels (inflating the $\beta_0$/$\beta_1$-driven proxy), whereas gravitationally organized matter concentrates into fewer, smoother, connected filaments and coherent voids. The real data scoring \emph{below} the mocks therefore indicates the pipeline is isolating organized large-scale structure rather than hallucinating topology from noise. We frame this as a validation of the \emph{algorithm's discriminating power} --- a necessary sanity condition --- and explicitly not as a measurement of extra-dimensional physics: any topology-sensitive statistic applied to a gravitationally clustered field versus a Poisson field is expected to show this asymmetry, independently of the K3 interpretation.
