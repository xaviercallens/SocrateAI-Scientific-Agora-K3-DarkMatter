\begin{scopebox}
This is a report on exact and numerical \emph{mathematics}. It contains no physical claim and no observable. The physical reading of
``$K3\times T^2$'' as a compactification, and any statement about a dark sector, are \tierC\ and are not tested, validated or
supported by anything here. No torus of any physical model is identified with an elliptic curve $E$ or $E'$. Nothing ranks the $s_7$
family against the $s_{10}$ family. No Kodaira fibre type is attached to any point of either family.
\end{scopebox}

\begin{abstract}
We collect the numerical results the programme has obtained for the Shioda--Inose reading of $K3\times T^2$ and say, for each, what
it is worth. Every table is generated from a certificate. The transcendental lattice of $E\times E'$ for a cyclic $n$-isogeny is
computed exactly in $\wedge^2\mathbb{Z}^4$ and is isometric to each family's certified lattice, for $n=7$ and $n=10$. At the three
Picard-number-$20$ points of $s_7$ the lattice of $E\times E$ for the computed CM order equals the certified row; that agreement is
forced by theory and is not evidence. A level-consistency gate between the lattice and modular legs holds for both families. At
$z=1/27$ the Mordell--Weil section demanded by the $M_7$ polarization is constructed by the Kumar--Kuwata isogeny graph, reconstructed
as a rational function from $52$ exact specializations (an over-determined fit with held-out points), and read: $\bar P\cdot\bar O=5$,
contact $0$ with the $A_1$ fibre, height $14$, equal to the lattice prediction. We then report, with counts and without re-deriving
them, what the DualScaleSimulator stream's own cross-check ledger records, and what its level experiments have and have not
established. The physical theory is not validated, and we state what a validation would require.
\end{abstract}

\section{The lattice of $E\times E'$}\label{sec:lattice}

Let $E\times E'=\mathbb{C}^2/\mathbb{Z}^4$. Then $H^2(E\times E',\mathbb{Z})\cong\wedge^2\mathbb{Z}^4$ with the pairing
$\langle u,w\rangle=$ the coefficient of $e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge e_4$ in $u\wedge w$, and a two-dimensional subtorus spanned
by $v_1,v_2$ has class $v_1\wedge v_2$. For curves without complex multiplication (an assumption, stated and not computed), the
N\'eron--Severi lattice is spanned by the two fibres and the graph of the isogeny. The checker verifies that this span has signature
$(1,2)$ and is saturated, computes its orthogonal complement as an integral basis, and finds an explicit unimodular basis change onto
the certified lattice. Controls that must fail do: a wrong level, a non-cyclic isogeny (caught by saturation), a reversed
orientation (caught by the signature), a tampered lattice, a wrong CM order, and a $j$-invariant that is not a class-number-one value.

which has no explicit model here, the reading holds at the lattice level only. The rows of $s_{10}$ at $\rho=20$ are not covered.

\section{The section at $z=1/27$}\label{sec:section}

At the three $\rho=20$ points of $s_7$ the two curves of the Inose model become isomorphic and the count of
\cref{tab:tw2} follows from exact reduction of the model and the height formula $h(P)=2\chi+2\,\bar P\cdot\bar O-\sum\mathrm{contr}$.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\input{tables/tw2_loci.tex}
\caption{The generic member and the three $\rho=20$ points of $s_7$. The point $z=\infty$ has Mordell--Weil rank $0$: there is no
section there.}\label{tab:tw2}
\end{table}

For $z=1/27$ the section is the one attached to the degree-$7$ endomorphism $\sqrt{-7}$ of $E$. We constructed it, rather than
inferring it from the lattice, by the following exact procedure.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The Vélu isogeny $\varphi\colon E_1\to E_2$ of degree $7$ is written over $\mathbb{Q}$ (kernel cubic, codomain), and
  $\varphi_y=\varphi_x'$ is checked exactly against the curve equation.
\item The cubic $C_t$ of Kumar--Kuwata (3.1) is reduced, through $s=x_2-t^2x_1$, to a quartic with a rational point, and mapped to a
  Weierstrass model $E_t$ by Mordell's formulas. The map is verified by identity on the actual divisor points, and $j(E_t)$ by the
  quartic's own invariants.
\item The divisors $D^{\pm}$ ($\varphi_y(x_1)=\pm t^3$, nine points) are summed by Riemann--Roch: the function in $L(10\,O)$ vanishing
  on $D^{\pm}$ has one further zero $R$, and $Q^{\pm}=-R$. The section is $P=Q^{+}-Q^{-}$.
\item $E_t$ is matched to the simple normal form $Y^2=X^3-3\alpha X+t_s+t_s^{-1}-2\beta$ ($\alpha=(J_1J_2)^{1/3}$, $\beta=J-1$,
  $t_s=t^6/343$, the constant $343=\sqrt{\Delta_{E_1}/\Delta_{E_2}}$ computed); the $j$-match and the twist constant are asserted at
  every specialization.
\item $X'(t_s)$ is reconstructed as a rational function from exact specializations and \emph{read}.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{headlinebox}
The explicit section at $z=1/27$ meets $O$ five times, once inside the $A_1$ fibre (hence on its identity component) and four times
at the roots of an irreducible quartic. Its height is $14=2\cdot7$, the value the lattice arithmetic and the Kumar--Kuwata theorem
both give. The two $E_8$ fibres carry no contact. \tierB.
\end{headlinebox}

\begin{remark}
A control found a robustness fact worth recording: $\bar P\cdot\bar O$ equals $(\deg N-4)/2$ whatever the denominator, because poles
that leave the finite line reappear at infinity. The \emph{count} is fixed by the degree of the numerator; the pole \emph{locations}
carry the contact information. Doubling the section does not fit the same shape, as it should (its height is four times larger).
\end{remark}

What this does not establish: that the section generates the whole Mordell--Weil group (no index computation was made; the lattice
side and the literature both give height $14$, which is the natural reading, not a proof here); anything at $z=-1$ (where the lattice
count has $\bar P\cdot\bar O=0$) or at $z=\infty$; or any physical statement.

\section{The simulation side: what the cross-check ledger records}\label{sec:sim}

The DualScaleSimulator stream maintains a ledger (its \texttt{k3t2\_rigidity\_v3} audit) comparing, for $332$ sealed targets of the
Lean library, values it computed in a separately written pipeline against the values stated in Lean. We report its \emph{counts},
recounted from its row-level data by a script that refuses on any mismatch, pinned to the source file's hash. We did not re-derive
its rows: nothing in \cref{tab:ledger} is verified by this stream, and the source's own header places its computations at tier~B at
best and its physical identifications at tiers~L and~C.

Two features of the table matter for how it may be used. First, most targets are not computed: the ledger is a map of what remains,
not a verdict on the library. Second, a fifth of the inputs it declares were typed from memory; the ledger says so itself, and a
number resting on such an input is only as good as the memory. Of the rigidity entries, three are confirmed, two are disputed
between its two reviewers and the rest are self-reported and unreviewed.

\paragraph{Its level experiments.} Quoting the simulator stream's own note (\texttt{audit/K3\_SELECTION.md}, not recomputed here):
the implemented model sits at level $12$ because a constant is hard-coded, so level $12$ is fitted, not forced; under the potential
actually integrated no observable depends on the level, so no level can be falsified; and a modular potential that does read the
level exists but is opt-in and not adopted. Its estimate is that, in one of its two modes, about three quarters of the apparent level
dependence of that potential is a stretched axis and not structure (over $14$ random points). The test that would show whether the
level reaches a reported observable through the dynamics (its item~L5) ran only partially and is neither met nor refuted.

\section{What cannot be concluded, and what would be needed}\label{sec:limits}

The physical theory is not validated. There is no worked matching from the geometric data to a four-dimensional effective theory,
the flux and tadpole route is blocked until a threefold base is specified, and the pre-registered prediction carries no derived
quantity. A validation would require, in order: a worked matching; a quantitative prediction that differs from $\Lambda$CDM; a
pre-registered pin; and survival against public data. None of the first link exists. The mathematics reported here is groundwork
for that chain and says nothing about whether it can be completed.

We did not compute $\operatorname{tr}G+\operatorname{tr}G^{-1}$ on the tori of the CM points. The Lean-proved dual-scale bound is a
statement about all positive-definite metrics; evaluating it at special points would need a typed K\"ahler modulus, is not invariant
under change of basis without an orbit minimum, would identify the torus of an elliptic curve with a physical torus, and would
order the two families. None of these is available.

\section{Corrections recorded in band}\label{sec:corrections}

\begin{correctionbox}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The reading of ``$K3\times T^2$'' used here was first described as the programme's choice before it had been ruled. It was
  ruled on 2026-10-08 (decision D29$'$, taken on the decision authority's behalf under explicit delegation) and is a convention for
  selection criteria only.
\item The first reconstruction of the section used a transcription of the Weierstrass form from a garbled line of the source. The
  exact $j$-match test refused it, and the simple normal form of the same source was used instead; the constant $t_s=t^6/343$ was
  then calibrated on three points and is asserted at all fifty-two.
\item A first sensitivity control for the read-off passed for the wrong reason (removing the quartic moves its poles to infinity,
  leaving the total unchanged). It was replaced by one that adds a genuine pole.
\item A note elsewhere in the programme said the host lacked a numerical library and that two test modules were ignored. That was a
  property of the system interpreter; under the project's own environment the full suite runs. Corrected 2026-10-10.
\item One continuous-integration run of the simulator failed once on a tolerance of $10^{-12}$ in an ODE-solve comparison and passed
  on re-run of the same commit; the tolerance is hardware-sensitive and was not changed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{correctionbox}
